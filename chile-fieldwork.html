Ecology and conservation in Northern Patagonia.

A six-week field-based study program across southern Chile, learning from research stations, host communities, and the landscapes that connect them.

Patagonian Ecosystems

Learning by living in the field.

Through Wildlands Studies’ Patagonian Ecosystems program, I spent six weeks based around Puerto Montt, Chile, studying ecology, conservation, and culture alongside 15 students from across the United States.

Guided by Chilean evolutionary biologist Darko Cotoras, PhD, whose research specializes in spiders, our group lived at research stations and camped in the field while learning from scientists, conservation practitioners, and local hosts.

Field-based study

Connecting questions to place.

Research

Collected and analyzed marine debris to study where it accumulated and how it was distributed along the coast.

Ecology

Presented on local flora and fauna, including Nothofagus pumilio and Austrochilidae spiders; studied annual forest regrowth and vegetation recovery after volcanic disturbance.

Stewardship & culture

Contributed to host-site maintenance, including firewood collection and boardwalk staining, and learned traditional food practices through a community-led curanto dinner.
